Prompt Engineering

Most advice about prompting is a list of tricks with no account of why any of them work. This unit starts from what the model is actually doing when it reads your text, derives the techniques from that, and then treats the result as what it is in production: a versioned artefact with tests, a cost, and a failure rate you can measure.

How to use this

Chapters 1–3 are the mechanism: what a prompt does to the model's output distribution, what every sampling knob changes, and how to lay a prompt out. Chapters 4–7 are the techniques, each derived from the mechanism rather than presented as folklore. Chapters 8–10 are about output you can depend on. Chapters 11–14 treat prompts as production code — versioned, tested, priced and portable.

Every chapter has a demo that computes rather than illustrates: a real softmax you can reshape with the sampling controls, a prompt linter, an example-selection bench, a significance check for A/B results. Code is Python and TypeScript. Arrow keys move between chapters.

No prior chapters required. If you want the surrounding system — retrieval, tool calling, deployment — that is the AI Engineer unit, and prompting inside a loop is AI Agents.

Two things this unit refuses to do

Give you a template library

Copyable "act as a…" templates transfer badly between models, tasks and versions. Every technique here comes with the reasoning that lets you derive the right prompt for your case, and the measurement that tells you whether it worked.

Claim prompting solves everything

Several chapters end by pointing out where a prompt is the wrong tool — where you need retrieval, a schema, code, or a different model. Knowing the ceiling of the technique is part of knowing the technique.
