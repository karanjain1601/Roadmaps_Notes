What a prompt actually is

Almost every piece of prompting advice is downstream of one fact: the model is not following your instructions, it is continuing your text. Once that lands, the techniques in the rest of this unit stop being a list of tricks and become predictable consequences.

conditioning distribution why it works limits

Continuation, not obedience

A language model does one thing: given a sequence of tokens, produce a probability distribution over what comes next. Every capability you have seen is that operation, repeated, with the output fed back in.

So a prompt is not a command. It is the left-hand side of a continuation — a piece of text chosen so that the most likely continuations are the ones you want. "Instruction following" is a behaviour that was trained in afterwards, by fine-tuning on examples where an instruction was followed by compliance. It is a strong tendency, not a mechanism, and it competes with every other pattern in the text.

The reframe that does the work

Stop asking "how do I tell the model what to do?" and start asking "what text makes the answer I want the most likely continuation?" Those sound similar and produce very different prompts. The second explains why examples beat descriptions, why a role changes the register, why "do not write X" often produces X, and why a well-chosen opening line to the assistant's turn is worth three paragraphs of instruction.

What follows from this

Six consequences. Each is a chapter later in this unit, and each is derivable from "the prompt conditions a distribution" without needing to be memorised.

ConsequenceWhy it followsChapter
Examples beat descriptionsAn example is the pattern. A description of a pattern is text about the pattern, which is a weaker conditioner than the thing itself05
Negations are unreliable"Do not mention pricing" puts pricing in the context, raising the probability of tokens near it. Saying what to do instead moves the mass where you want it04
Format is contagiousIf your examples use bullet points, bullets become likely. This is useful, and it is also how examples leak content you did not intend05
Position mattersAttention is not uniform over the context. The start and end are attended to most reliably, so constraints buried in the middle of a long prompt are weaker03
Thinking out loud helpsEach token is computed from those before it. Reasoning tokens are extra computation, and they place intermediate results in the context where later tokens can attend to them07
Style transfers with substanceConditioning on "a senior lawyer wrote this" shifts vocabulary, hedging and structure together, because those co-occur in the training text06

Three layers, one string

By the time it reaches the model, everything is one token sequence. The role labels are formatting the provider applies, and they matter only because the model was trained to treat them as meaningful.

what the model actually receives, roughly
<|system|>You are a support assistant for Northwind. Answer in under 40 words.<|end|>
<|user|>How do I cancel my subscription?<|end|>
<|assistant|>

              ↑ generation starts here, conditioned on everything above

Two practical implications. First, the system role is a convention, not a privilege level — models are trained to weight it heavily, and they mostly do, but it is tokens in the same stream as everything else. That is the root of chapter 10. Second, everything is append-only during generation: the model cannot revise a token it has already emitted, which is why an early wrong turn tends to be defended rather than corrected, and why prefilling the first few words is so effective.

Prefill: the technique that surprises people

Where the provider allows it, you can start the assistant's turn yourself. Supplying {" makes valid JSON overwhelmingly likely. Supplying Step 1: forces enumerated reasoning. Supplying Based on the sources provided, makes an ungrounded answer awkward to continue into. You are not instructing — you are placing the generation somewhere in the space where the continuations you want are the probable ones. It costs nothing and often replaces a paragraph of instruction.

The limits of the technique

A unit on prompting should be honest about the ceiling. Prompting shifts probability; it does not add capability, information or guarantees.

No prompt will fix

  • Missing knowledge. The model cannot know your refund policy. Retrieve it
  • Missing capability. If a model cannot do the reasoning, phrasing will not unlock it
  • Needing a guarantee. "Always return valid JSON" is a strong tendency, not a contract. Constrain the decoding and validate
  • Arithmetic and exact computation. Give it a calculator
  • Current facts. Search
  • A security boundary. Chapter 10

Prompting is the right tool for

  • Format, structure and register
  • Which of several plausible behaviours you get
  • Making implicit task requirements explicit
  • Trading verbosity, latency and accuracy against each other
  • Eliciting reasoning the model can do but does not by default
  • Reducing a failure rate from 12% to 2% — the last 2% is engineering
Prompting is the cheapest lever, not the only one

It is free to try, instant to iterate and needs no infrastructure, which is exactly why teams over-invest in it. When a prompt has been through six revisions and the failure rate is still 8%, that is usually the technique telling you it has run out — and the next move is retrieval, a schema, a tool, decomposition into two calls, or a different model. Recognising the ceiling early is worth more than one more rewrite.

A worked reframing

Written as a command

Summarise this support ticket. Don't be too long. Don't include irrelevant details. Make sure it's useful for the agent.

Three negations, one vague quality bar, no format. The words "long", "irrelevant" and "useful" are in the context but nothing indicates what the output should look like.

Written as a continuation

Summarise this support ticket for an agent picking it up cold.

Format:
Issue: <one sentence>
Customer wants: <one sentence>
Already tried: <bullets, or "nothing">
Blocked on: <one sentence, or "nothing">

Ticket:
<<<{ticket}>>>

The format is the constraint. Length, relevance and usefulness all follow from the shape rather than from adjectives about it.

Note what changed. Nothing was added about being concise — the four-field template makes verbosity structurally awkward. Nothing forbids irrelevant detail — there is nowhere to put it. This is the general move: replace adjectives with structure, because structure conditions the distribution and adjectives mostly do not.

The short version

  1. A model produces a distribution over the next token. A prompt is text chosen so the continuations you want are the likely ones.
  2. Instruction-following is a trained tendency, not a mechanism — it competes with every other pattern in the text.
  3. Ask "what makes my answer the probable continuation?" rather than "how do I say what I want?"
  4. Six familiar rules — examples beat descriptions, negations misfire, format is contagious, position matters, thinking helps, style carries substance — all follow from that one fact.
  5. Everything is one token stream. The system role is a convention, not a privilege level.
  6. Generation is append-only, which is why prefilling the first few words is so effective.
  7. Prompting cannot supply knowledge, capability or guarantees. Recognise the ceiling instead of rewriting a seventh time.
  8. The general move: replace adjectives with structure.
