Everything between a transistor and a distributed system, in the order the ideas actually build on each other. Start at the machine, climb through the operating system, learn to measure cost, then meet the structures and algorithms that cost was invented to describe.

Reading order

The parts are cumulative. Chapters 1–9 explain what the hardware and operating system are doing under every program you write; 10–11 give you the vocabulary for cost; 12–23 are the classic structures and algorithms; 24–30 zoom out to theory and whole systems.

If you only have an afternoon

Chapters 10, 12, 13 and 18 carry the most weight per page: complexity, the two ways to lay out a sequence, hashing, and graphs. Almost every interview question and half of everyday engineering sits on those four.

How the demos work

Each one runs the real algorithm in your browser — no recorded animation, no hard-coded numbers. Change an input and the readouts recompute. If a demo disagrees with the prose above it, the prose is the bug.
