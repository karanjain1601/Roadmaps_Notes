Node.js

Twenty chapters on a runtime whose entire character comes from one design decision: your JavaScript runs on a single thread, and almost nothing else does. Every strength Node has and every way it fails follows from that, and the chapters here are largely about making the consequence visible.

Its relationship to the other units

The Backend Developer unit covers the framework-neutral concepts — HTTP semantics, indexes, isolation levels, caching strategies, circuit breakers. This unit assumes those and covers what is specific to Node: the runtime's concurrency model, its module system, its streams, its ecosystem. Where a chapter says "chapter 10 of the Backend unit", the concept lives there. The Spring Boot unit is the same exercise for the JVM, and comparing chapter 3 here with chapter 7 there is the fastest way to understand what each runtime is actually trading.
