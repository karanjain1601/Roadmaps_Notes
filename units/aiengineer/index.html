How to use this

The chapters are ordered as a build. Chapters 1–3 give you the mental model, 4–8 get a model answering properly in your own code, 9–13 teach it your data, 14–16 give it autonomy and senses, and 17–20 are what stands between a demo and something you would put your name on.

Code is Python and TypeScript, because that is what the ecosystem is written in. Every technique gets a worked example, the numbers behind it, and the failure that makes people abandon it. Arrow keys move between chapters.

Model names, prices and context limits move fast. Anything that dates quickly is written as a method you can re-run against today's numbers rather than a figure to memorise.

What this deliberately leaves out

Training models from scratch

Backpropagation, optimiser choice and pre-training runs belong to the Machine Learning and AI & Data Scientist units. You need to know what the model does, not how to build one.

Serving infrastructure at scale

GPU cluster operations, autoscaling inference fleets and model registries are MLOps territory. Chapter 8 covers self-hosting up to the point where it becomes an ops problem.

Frontend and backend basics

You are assumed to know HTTP, async, a database and a deploy pipeline. Where an AI feature bends those (streaming, long requests, queueing) it is covered in chapter 19.
