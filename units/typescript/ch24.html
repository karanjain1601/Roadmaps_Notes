5Where to go next

Three directions, depending on what you found hard.

If Part 4 was the difficult part
That is the expected outcome, and Chapter 17's section 5 is the reassurance: most application code needs discriminated unions, branded types and satisfies, and very little else. Read library types when they break, and do not write recursive ones.
If the boundaries were the revelation
Chapter 20 is the one with the largest practical payoff per line of code. Pick one service in your codebase, put a schema at its edge, derive the type from it, and see how many ! and as further in become unnecessary.
If you want to keep going
The compiler's own source is unusually readable, and lib.es5.d.ts is the single best-annotated file in the ecosystem. Type-level puzzle sets are good exercise and poor guidance about production code — enjoy them as puzzles.
And the one habit worth keeping
When a type is hard to write, ask whether the value is hard to think about. A great deal of type-level difficulty is a data model asking to be changed, and Chapter 17's state-space count is the fastest way to find out.

The whole unit, in five lines

Types are erased and structural, so nothing survives to runtime and shapes matter rather than names. strict is the product; the flags are the reason. Narrowing is the everyday tool and generics relate positions. Parse at the boundary, because that is where the types stop being true. And a cast's honesty is its distance from its justification.
