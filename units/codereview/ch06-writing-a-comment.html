On work alone, labelling saves 20% — the author skips five non-blocking comments worth 18 minutes. Modest.

On rounds it is the difference between 42 hours of waiting and 6, a sevenfold reduction, and author-minutes fall from 172 to 82. Most of that is not the comments at all: seven rounds × 12 minutes is 84 minutes spent remembering the change rather than improving it.

The mechanism is worth stating plainly. An unlabelled review invites a clarifying round — "which of these block?" — before any work happens. A labelled one does not.

A result I got backwards

I expected more comments to show diminishing returns — that a reviewer leaving forty comments would be less efficient than one leaving two. Modelled, the opposite happens.

Why it rises rather than falls

The 12-minute context switch is a fixed cost per round, not per comment. Spreading it over forty comments instead of two makes each comment cheaper to act on — so a thorough single-pass review is 42% more efficient per author-hour than a brief one.

Which means there is no efficiency argument for holding comments back. A reviewer who notices twenty things and mentions twelve is not being kind; they are wasting a round they have already paid for, and the other eight will surface later at full price.

The number of comments is not the problem. Unlabelled and trickled is the problem — and forty comments delivered in one labelled pass is still exactly one round, the same as two.

Five forms, and what each invites

The same objection, written five ways
FormExampleInvites
Instruction"Change this to X."Compliance, or a fight
Question"Why X rather than Y?"An explanation you may not have
Evidence"This breaks when n is 0."A fix, with no argument available
Advisory"Consider X. Non-blocking."A judgement, and permission to decline
Noise"I'd have done X, but this is fine."Nothing — delete it
Evidence is the strongest form because it removes the reviewer

"This breaks when n is 0" cannot be argued with and needs no authority. The reviewer's seniority stops mattering entirely, which is exactly what you want — chapter 14 is largely about disagreements that would not have happened if someone had produced a failing input instead of an opinion.

The question form's real use is narrower than its reputation. It is for when you might be wrong: asking costs one round if your suspicion is right and saves a wasted rewrite if it is not. Used as a softener for an instruction you have already decided on — "have you considered doing it correctly?" — it is worse than the instruction, because now the author has to decode it too.

The last row is worth deleting on sight. A comment that changes nothing and requires a reply costs a round trip to say "yes, fine".

Check yourself

What does labelling severity actually save?

Rounds, not work. On work alone it saves about 20% — the author skips five non-blocking comments worth 18 minutes. On rounds it is the difference between 42 hours of waiting and 6, with author-minutes falling from 172 to 82. Most of that gap is not the comments: seven rounds at 12 minutes of context switching is 84 minutes spent remembering the change rather than improving it.

Why does an unlabelled review cost an extra round even when batched?

Because the author cannot tell what blocks, so either they address everything — 7 comments instead of 2 — or they ask which ones matter, and that question is a round. The model charges the batched-but-unlabelled case two rounds for exactly this reason: one to clarify and one to act. Labelling removes the clarifying round entirely, which is why it is worth more than the work it saves.

How far does comment discipline reach?

Two chapters. Time-to-merge is the review latency in chapter 4's fixed point, so a 6-hour merge settles diffs at 75 lines with a 100% catch rate and a 42-hour merge settles them at 525 lines with 38%. That is 62 points of catch rate decided by how comments are delivered — and the loop closes, because a bigger diff produces more comments.

Is a reviewer who leaves 40 comments less efficient than one who leaves 2?

No — 42% more efficient per author-hour, which is the opposite of what I expected. The 12-minute context switch is a fixed cost per round, not per comment, so spreading it over forty comments makes each cheaper to act on: 1.62 defects per author-hour at two comments against 2.31 at forty. There is no efficiency argument for a shorter review.

Is it kind to mention only the most important twelve of twenty findings?

No — it wastes a round you have already paid for. The eight you held back either surface in a later round, costing another context switch each, or never get fixed. If they are worth saying, say them now and label them non-blocking; if they are not worth saying, they will not be worth saying next week either. Forty comments in one labelled pass is still exactly one round, the same as two.

What is the strongest form for a review comment?

Evidence. "This breaks when n is 0" cannot be argued with and needs no authority — the reviewer's seniority stops mattering entirely, which is what you want. Most review disagreements are ones that would not have happened if someone had produced a failing input instead of an opinion. Instruction invites compliance or a fight; opinion turns a fact into a seniority contest.

When should you use the question form?

When you might be wrong. Asking costs one round if your suspicion is right and saves a wasted rewrite if it is not. Its reputation as a politeness device is where it goes bad: used as a softener for an instruction you have already decided on — "have you considered doing it correctly?" — it is worse than the instruction, because the author now has to decode it before acting on it.

Ten things to carry forward

  1. Comment costs span 90-fold, from a 2-minute nit to a 180-minute rewrite.
  2. Every round costs 12 minutes of context switching regardless of its contents.
  3. Labelling saves 20% of work and 85% of waiting. The rounds are the prize.
  4. Unlabelled invites a clarifying round even when the comments arrive together.
  5. 7 rounds against 1: 42 hours against 6.
  6. That reaches chapter 4: 62 points of catch rate from delivery alone.
  7. Thoroughness is 42% MORE efficient, because the round cost is fixed.
  8. Holding comments back is not kindness — it defers them at full price.
  9. Evidence beats opinion because it removes the reviewer from the argument.
  10. The question form is for when you might be wrong, not for softening.

About the model

Comment costs, the per-round context switch and the blocking fraction are parameters, visible in the demos. The conclusions — that labelling saves rounds rather than work, and that thoroughness is cheaper per author-hour — follow from the fixed per-round cost, which is the assumption to argue with if you disagree.
