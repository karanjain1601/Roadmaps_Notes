Code Review

Forty-two percent of review comments are about formatting. The two layers that decide whether the code can be changed next year get eleven and twenty-four. This unit is about moving that attention — and about the two defect classes review catches five percent of the time, which no amount of moving will fix.

16 chapters 4 parts 2 interactive demos per chapter Offline — no server needed

Each chapter takes one pattern: the phrasing that signals it, the template, two or three canonical problems worked through, the mistakes that cost people the offer, and the complexity you should be able to state before writing a line. Every demo runs the real algorithm and checks its answer against brute force, so you can watch a pattern break on the input it was never suited to.

How this differs from the DSA unit

That one teaches the structures: how a heap works, why a hash table collides, what a trie costs. This one assumes you have them and asks a different question — given three sentences of prose about a delivery schedule, which structure did they just describe, and how do you know before you have finished reading?

Reading order

Chapters 1 and 2 are about practice technique and are worth reading first even if you skip around afterwards. Parts 2 to 4 are the patterns that appear most often; part 5 is graphs; part 6 is where the difficulty lives; part 7 covers the remainder plus the interview itself.

How the demos work

Each one runs the real algorithm in your browser and, where an answer can be checked, verifies it against exhaustive search on every input. No recorded animations and no hard-coded numbers. If a demo disagrees with the prose above it, the prose is the bug.
