    The window is not a convenience setting

    Chapter 12 established that a change smaller than the noise cannot dominate the chart at any axis setting. So the default window, which sets the noise floor, decides which questions the dashboard is capable of answering.

    A 7-day default has a 7.94% floor and can show two of those four changes. A 90-day default has a 2.21% floor and shows three. Nobody experiences this as an analytical decision, and it is one.

    Aggregating attenuates by exactly the segment's share

    This is chapter 3's elasticity result arriving as a layout problem: a lever's effect on the total equals its effect within the segment times that segment's share, exactly.

    So the enterprise segment needs a 66% effect to be visible on the default view. Nothing that large happens. Which means the all-users default will never surface an enterprise problem — not rarely, never — and enterprise is usually the segment people most want to watch.

    What to do about it

    Make the default the segment you care about, not the union of everything. A dashboard that opens on "all users" is optimised for the case where every segment behaves the same, which is the case where you would not need a dashboard.

    Or show small multiples: one small chart per segment, rather than one aggregate chart. That costs vertical space and removes the attenuation entirely, because each panel has its own denominator.

    Drilling one dimension at a time is D×k, not kᵏ

    Four dimensions of six values is 1,296 cells and 24 marginal views — a 54-fold saving. That is what a drill path buys, and it follows from the arithmetic rather than from anyone's taste in layout.

    It is also why a dashboard with a well-chosen hierarchy feels so much better than one with the same tiles arranged flat. The hierarchy is not decoration; it is the difference between 24 things to look at and 1,296.

    Not "hard to spot" — arithmetically invisible

    From three dimensions upward, a cell going to zero moves its margin by 2.78% against a 3.97% noise floor. There is no effect size large enough to fix this, because total loss is the maximum a decline can be.

    So "the dashboard looked fine" and "one region on one product on mobile was broken for a fortnight" are not in tension. The margin arithmetic guarantees they can both be true.

    Which is a real limit on what drill-down can do, and it is the opposite of how drill-down is usually sold. It finds main effects reliably and it cannot find interactions at all.

    Interaction-hunting is a job for a query, not a dashboard

    A 1% volume floor leaves 100 cells worth checking of 1,296 — a tractable automated sweep and an intractable manual one.

    So the honest division of labour is: the dashboard tells you something changed, and a scheduled query that sweeps every cell above a volume floor tells you where. Expecting a human to find an interaction by clicking through margins is asking them to beat arithmetic.

    TWO DIFFERENT ARTEFACTS

    Monitoring against analysis

    They want opposite things
    MonitoringAnalysis
    QuestionIs something wrong?Why?
    Read forSeconds, dailyTwenty minutes, once
    TilesFew, fixed, glanceableMany, filterable
    Default windowLong enough to be quietWhatever the question needs
    SegmentsSmall multiples, no aggregationSelectable
    SuccessNothing needs doing, and you knew in five secondsYou leave with an answer
    Most dashboards are one artefact trying to be both

    The tell is a monitoring dashboard with forty tiles and a date picker. Forty tiles cannot be glanced at, and chapter 10 priced the alarm side of that: sixty metrics with limits give a false alarm every six weeks.

    Splitting them costs one extra page and makes both work. The monitoring page carries the six numbers that would change someone's day, at a window long enough to be quiet. The analysis page carries everything else, and nobody opens it daily.

  1. Default to the segment you care about, not the union. Aggregation attenuates by exactly the segment share.
  2. Choose the default window from the change worth seeing. It sets the noise floor and therefore the question set.
  3. Small multiples beat a filter dropdown for monitoring, because nobody changes a default and every panel keeps its own denominator.
  4. Put the drill hierarchy in the layout, in the order causes usually live. 24 views against 1,296.
  5. Schedule a cell sweep for interactions. The margins cannot find them, and 100 cells is a cheap query.
  6. Show freshness and completeness per source, as chapter 9 measured — the aggregate figure cannot distinguish 5% from 32%.
  7. Delete tiles nobody opens. Chapter 1 found 103 of 200 dashboards never opened; the same is true of tiles within one.

    8. JUDGEMENT

    Five questions about a dashboard

  8. What is the default window, and what noise floor does it imply? That is the smallest question it can answer.
  9. Does it default to an aggregate over segments? A 30% effect in a 6% segment shows as 1.8%.
  10. How many dimensions deep are your causes? From three up, margins cannot find a single-cell cause.
  11. Is it monitoring or analysis? If the answer is "both", it is probably neither.
  12. Which tiles were opened last month? The unopened ones cost load time and attention for nothing.
