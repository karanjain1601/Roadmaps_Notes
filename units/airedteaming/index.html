AI Red Teaming

Adversarial testing of AI systems, written from the defender's side. The interesting part is not that models can be tricked — it is that a system built around one collapses several security boundaries that conventional software keeps separate, and the engineering response to that is specific and mostly under-applied.

What this is, and what it is not

This is a unit about building AI systems that survive contact with adversaries. It explains attack classes at the level of mechanism — why a category of attack works, what it exploits, what it can reach — because that is what you need to design a control that holds. It does not supply working payloads, bypass templates or exploit code, and it is not a jailbreak collection. That is a deliberate choice rather than squeamishness: a list of prompts is obsolete in a month and teaches nothing about why the class exists, whereas understanding the mechanism tells you what to test and what to build.

Two prerequisites. You should understand roughly how a language model works — tokens, context, instruction-following; the AI Engineer and Prompt Engineering units cover that ground. And you should have some security grounding: the Cyber Security and DevSecOps units are referenced throughout, because a great deal of AI security is ordinary security applied to a new component, and confusing the novel parts with the familiar ones is the commonest analytical error in the field.

Chapters 1–3 are the framing, and chapter 4 is the one to read twice: it is the structural fact from which the whole prompt-layer part follows. Chapters 5–7 are the prompt layer, 8–11 the model and its data, 12–14 the systems built around it — including agents, where a text vulnerability becomes an action — and 15–18 are how the work is actually done and reported.

Two commitments

Mechanisms, not payloads

Every attack chapter answers three questions: what does this exploit, what can it reach, and what control actually stops it. Where a concrete example clarifies a mechanism it is included in a form that illustrates rather than operates. The test applied throughout is whether a defender could build the right control from the explanation.

Honest about what does not work

A great deal of AI security marketing implies solved problems. Prompt injection is not solved. Adversarial robustness costs accuracy and generalises poorly. Machine unlearning is a research area, not a tool. Where the honest answer is architectural containment rather than a fix, the chapter says so rather than recommending a filter.
