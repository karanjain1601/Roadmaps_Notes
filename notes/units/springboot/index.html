Spring Boot

Twenty chapters on the framework that runs a very large share of the world's backends. Spring's reputation is that it is magic — annotations that do things nobody can trace. This unit is largely about removing that impression: every piece of the magic is a proxy, a conditional bean or a servlet filter, and once you can see which, the framework becomes predictable.

Its relationship to the Backend unit

The Backend Developer unit covers the concepts — HTTP semantics, indexes, isolation levels, caching strategies, circuit breakers — without reference to a framework. This unit assumes those and covers how Spring does them, and where its defaults differ from what you would choose. Where a chapter here says "chapter 10 of the Backend unit", it means the concept lives there and only the Spring-specific part is repeated.
